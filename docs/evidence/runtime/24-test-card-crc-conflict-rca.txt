3. MECHANISM: HOW TWELVE FRAMES BECAME THREE CRC VALUES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

One chain, five links.

L1  The driver enables the CRTC for its in-kernel fbdev client's all-zero
    fbcon buffer (F14, F22). The frame is blank (F4), `test_pattern=auto`
    therefore paints (F3), and `sm750_pipe_enable()` writes the card at
    `scanout_offset` 0 (F5, F6), sets `test_pattern_active` and
    `test_pattern_fresh`, logs the paint line, and schedules a 5000 ms rotation
    (F5). This is the 22:13:33 "painted a test card" line.

L2  The check's client then issues one `drmModeSetCrtc` on the already-active
    CRTC (F17, F18). That is an update commit: no `pipe_disable`, no
    `pipe_enable`, so neither the card nor the rotation is touched by the modeset
    hooks.

L3  That update commit reaches `sm750_pipe_update()`, which hits the
    `test_pattern_fresh` guard (F9) and returns via `goto event` **without
    uploading anything**. The guard was set by L1 and consumed here, one commit
    after it was set (see section 7). Because the skip precedes
    `sm750_test_pattern_cancel()` (:3882), the rotation is also **not**
    cancelled, and `test_pattern_active` stays true for the rest of the module
    load.

L4  The scanout window therefore keeps carrying card content while the CRC
    source is armed (F16 :191). It is rewritten by up to three independent
    writers, all in place at the live `scanout_offset`:
      * the rotation work every `test_pattern_period_ms` (5000 ms) - F7, F6;
      * every later fbdev re-enable, which re-runs the whole paint block
        (`should_paint(blank)` is still true because the plane buffer is still
        all-zero: F3, F4, F5) - this is the 22:13:39 paint, section 6;
      * `sm750_crc_resume()` after such an enable re-arms the stream, so
        sampling can also continue across a modeset.
    Each paint is a 1080-row sequential MMIO burst over exactly the bytes the
    CRC reader is walking (F6, F11).

L5  A CRC entry is not atomic. `sm750_scanout_crc32()` (F11) walks 262144
    bytes in 256-byte `memcpy_fromio` chunks - 1024 reads per entry - while the
    paint writes the same 262144 bytes top-to-bottom. If one entry's walk
    overlaps a paint, its CRC is a mixture of old and new content: distinct
    from the pre-paint value and from the post-paint value. The stream then
    contains three distinct CRC values and exactly one of them (the torn one)
    is unique:
      V1 = card, phase n        (entries before the paint)
      V2 = card, phase n, torn  (the entry that straddled the paint)
      V3 = card, phase n+1      (entries after the paint)
    `12 frames` at 16.6 ms is ~200 ms (F13) and the sample is taken from a
    stream that has already been running across L1..L3, so the window in which
    a repaint must fall is wide with respect to a paint that takes a few
    milliseconds.

Why exactly three and not two or many: the repaint is a single pass over the
window, so at most one entry per repaint can be torn. The check fails on
`uniq != 1`, so it reports 3 (V1, V2, V3) whenever one paint falls inside the
sample and the sample covers content from both sides of it. Three is the
minimum signature of "one in-place repaint overlapped one CRC read"; it is not
evidence of three paint sources (see section 6 for the second, independent
source that the same defect arms).

Why the check's own assertions make this deterministic rather than random: the
`draw black` + `sleep 1` at :189 plus the 4-entry warm-up at :205 place the
12-entry sample roughly 1.0-1.3 s after the client's commit, and the paint and
rotation schedule is unbounded in time, so the overlap appears on whichever run
a repaint lands in that ~200 ms.

The accompanying content evidence is decisive and independent of timing: the
post-run readback is `offset 0`, `nonzero 200459`, `ff ff ff ff`. The client's
black frame (a) never left the shadow buffer because of L3, and (b) its upload,
had it run, would have gone to `backbuffer_offset` 4147200 and flipped the
scanout there (F9). The window still held the card. That is the same defect
seen in its steady-state form instead of its torn form.

4. CANDIDATE CAUSES, ENUMERATED AND FALSIFIED OR CONFIRMED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

(a) THE 5 s ROTATION TICK REPAINTING MID-MEASUREMENT.
    Verdict: CONFIRMED as one of the repaint sources, and FALSIFIED as a
    standalone cause. The rotation cannot on its own produce three values in
    twelve frames: 12 frames span ~200 ms (F13), not 5 s, and the check's
    timings (F16: `sleep 1`, 4-entry warm-up, 20 s `timeout`) never stretch a
    12-entry sample to 5 s. What it does instead is *arm* the defect: after the
    rotation is scheduled at L1 and never cancelled at L3, every run carries a
    repaint source whose phase the check cannot predict. Note the general case
    is worse than 5 s: a mode change during sampling (F12 suspend/resume) can
    pause and resume the stream and stretch the sample's wall-clock, which is
    why the overlap is not a rare accident.

(b) A STAGED FLIP MOVING `scanout_offset` TO 4147200 WHILE THE WINDOW IS READ
    Verdict: FALSIFIED for this run; it is a real second-order hazard but not
    the observed mechanism.
    * No commit happens inside the measured window: the client commits once
      before sampling and then sleeps 45 s (F17); the sample is a pure reader.
    * In this run the very code that would flip was skipped: `pipe_update`
      returned at the `test_pattern_fresh` guard before the shadow path (F9), so
      no upload and no flip ran for the client's commit at all.
    * The readback proves no flip won: `offset 0` (F11 recomputes offset from
      the live field each call, so the readback reports the live value), while a
      successful staged flip leaves `scanout_offset == 4147200` (F9). F19 shows
      what a flip looks like when it works (offset 4147200).
    * However the hazard is real in general: were the flip to run, the window
      offset would change between two consecutive CRC entries, which also yields
      three values (old window, one entry read across the address change, new
      window). That is why the fix in section 8 must make the CRC consumer stop
      the card rather than merely repaint less often: both (a) and (b) are
      symptoms of "the window can be rewritten or moved while a consumer is
      sampling it".
    * The 4 MB `full-frame backbuffer staging` line is not itself a flip; it is
      reported from `pipe_enable()` (F5) and only announces that the back buffer
      is usable.

(c) THE `test_pattern_fresh` SINGLE-SKIP GUARD COVERING THE ENABLING COMMIT BUT
    NOT THE SUBSEQUENT COMMIT, SO CARD AND CLIENT FRAME ALTERNATE.
    Verdict: CONFIRMED, and it is the direct cause of the *content* failure
    (`nonzero 200459`, the client's black frame absent). Half of the parent's
    hypothesis is wrong and the correction matters for the fix: the guard does
    **not** cover the enabling commit. `drm_atomic_helper_commit_tail()` runs
    planes before modeset enables (F15), so `pipe_update()` for the enabling
    commit has already run - and returned early for the pre-paint state - before
    `pipe_enable()` sets `test_pattern_fresh` at :3792. The flag is therefore
    consumed by the *next* commit, which is the client's first real frame in
    this run (L2/L3) and, in general, whatever commit comes next.
    Consequence, proven by the readback: the client's frame is dropped, the card
    stays on screen, and the rotation that `pipe_update` would have cancelled at
    :3882 is never cancelled. "Alternate" is too generous - the client's frame
    never appears at all.
    Also confirmed: this is not a benign one-shot skip; the same
    guard drops *any* first frame that follows an enable while a card is
    painted, including a real client's.

(d) `sm750_crc_region()` CACHING AN OFFSET THAT IS NOW STALE
    Verdict: FALSIFIED. `sm750_crc_region()` :3104-3130 assigns
    `*offset = sdev->scanout_offset;` on every call and caches nothing (F11).
    The debugfs readback path calls the same function and the same
    `sm750_scanout_crc32()` (:4128-4146), so a stale offset cannot make the two
    disagree, and it cannot produce three distinct values from static content:
    the offset is constant across the whole sample unless a flip happens, and
    (b) rules a flip in. Consistent with this, the readback's
    `length 262144` equals `crc_window_bytes` (F1), i.e. the region computed
    fine, and its `crc32 0x45c3bf90` is a card image, not a wrong-address read
    (a wrong address would not be a plausible card picture with white rows).

(e) ANYTHING ELSE
    e1  A stalled or double-armed vblank timer: FALSIFIED. `sm750_crc_work_fn`
        re-arms at count+1 (F12) and 12 entries did arrive - the failure is
        :215 (unique count), not :209/:214 (missing entries). A stall would
        also leave the CRC list short and the check would have said so.
    e2  The CRC list carrying entries older than the sample: the sample reads
        the *tail* of the list (`head -n (n+1) | tail -n +2`, F16) and discards a
        4-entry warm-up, so entries predating the arm are not in the counted 12.
        Also FALSIFIED as the mechanism: the stream is monotonic and one entry
        per vblank (F12).
    e3  Colour-depth/format conversion making a static frame vary:
        FALSIFIED. Pre-T37 the same path produced a stable 0xe20eea22 for a
        static black frame (F19) and a stable 0x06cf606c for white; nothing in
        the conversion path writes the window after a commit.
    e4  A second, independent repaint writer: CONFIRMED, see section 6. The
        driver's own fbdev client re-commits (and therefore repaints) seconds
        after the first enable. This does not change the mechanism; it changes
        the probability and explains why the defect reproduces on this host
        rather than only in theory.
    e5  The check reading the wrong field (`$NF` parsing the frame number):
        FALSIFIED. The line format on this kernel is "%u %08x" and `$NF` is the
        CRC; the same parse produced the correct constants in F19 and the
        recorded 0xe20eea22. A frame number as "CRC" would have been stable too.

5. ANSWER TO "WHY A SECOND ENABLE SIX SECONDS LATER"
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The dmesg pair at 22:13:39 is a genuine second `sm750_pipe_enable()`: the
"full-frame backbuffer staging enabled" and "painted a test card" lines exist
only inside that function's success path (F5 :3760-3764 and :3794). Candidates:

  * The check's client did a modeset that re-enabled the CRTC. FALSIFIED.
    The client's single `drmModeSetCrtc` runs after 22:13:39 (it is started by
    the check, which runs after the cycle script's geometry wait, F18), it keeps
    the mode already on screen (F17), and it is an update commit - proven by
    F19: pre-T37 the same client never produced a staging line, and its frame
    landed by in-place upload at offset 0 in one run and by flip in another.
  * A lastclose restore of a userspace master. FALSIFIED as the actor here: no
    userspace master existed before the check started (F22) and src/ has no
    `.lastclose`/`.master_set` hook; the generic DRM lastclose path needs a
    master that closed, and none had.
  * The in-kernel fbdev client re-committing. CONFIRMED, by elimination and by
    three independent observations:
      1. It is the same driver-side actor that owns every enable at load with no
         userspace master (F22), and repeated enables in a burst at load were
         already recorded pre-T37 (F21: four staging lines at 1497.46, 1500.14,
         1502.95, 1506.83 with no client present).
      2. The fbdev client demonstrably issues a restore commit on its own after
         a userspace client detaches (F20) - so it is an active committer, not a
         one-shot at probe.
      3. The ~2 s gaps in F21 are the SiI9024A link-wait inside its prepare path;
         the 5-7 s gap here is the same retry behaviour at a slower point
         (the 22:13:39 enable follows the 22:13:33 one by 5.1-6.9 s, and dmesg
         shows no user-space actor between them).
    The second paint re-ran because `sm750_shadow_frame_is_blank()` still
    reported blank (F4): the plane's buffer is the same all-zero fbcon buffer a
    new enable is handed, so `should_paint(blank)` is true again (F3), the
    card is repainted with the *current* phase (`test_pattern_active` was never
    cleared, F7), `test_pattern_fresh` is re-armed, and the rotation is
    re-scheduled.
  * Did T37 *introduce* the second enable? No. F21 shows the same multi-enable
    signature in 0.6.0. What T37 changed is the consequence: before T37 a
    re-enable only reset the scanout to a blank buffer that was already blank,
    so a CRC consumer saw no visible change; after T37 each re-enable performs a
    4 MB in-place repaint of the armed window, re-arms the fresh guard and
    restarts the rotation. T37 turned a harmless re-commit into a destructive
    one, and made it visible with a new drm_info line.

OPEN (needs root): which fbdev-client path triggered the 22:13:39 commit
(hotplug re-probe versus a restore after a failed probe retry). Closing it is a
one-liner on the host:

    sudo -n dmesg | grep -nE 'sm750|fb0|sii902|drm_client' | sed -n '/22:13:3/,/22:13:4/p'

It does not affect the diagnosis or the fix: either path re-enters
`sm750_pipe_enable()`, which is what the fix has to make safe.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. IS `test_pattern_fresh` CORRECT IN GENERAL?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

No. It is wrong in three separable ways, and it currently masks a wider ordering
defect between the paint and the client's first upload.

6.1 It is consumed one commit too late. Because planes are committed before
    modeset enables (F15), `pipe_enable()` sets the flag *after* the enabling
    commit's own plane update has run. Whatever commit comes next loses its
    upload - here the client's first frame (L3, proven by the readback), in
    general any commit: a client's first present, a damage update from the
    fbdev client, or a cursor-driven update. The comment at :3791 ("The enabling
    commit also runs sm750_pipe_update()") is factually inverted for the default
    commit tail.

6.2 It returns before the cancel. `goto event` at :3875 jumps over
    `sm750_test_pattern_cancel()` at :3882, so the one hook that is supposed to
    end the card ("a client produced a frame") is skipped exactly when it is
    first needed. The card then survives until `pipe_disable()` (:3925) or
    module removal (:4669), i.e. potentially for the whole session, repainting
    every 5 s under whatever the panel shows.

6.3 The trigger for painting is wrong. `should_paint(blank)` treats an all-zero
    client frame as "no client" (F3, F4). `tests/scanout-pattern.c` black is a
    legitimate frame; so is a black X screen, a blanked screensaver, a paused
    video and a VT with a black background. The correct predicate for "nobody
    is producing frames" is "no userspace DRM master exists"
    (`READ_ONCE(sdev->drm.master)` - the driver already relies on the fbdev
    client being the only master, F22), optionally refined by the fbdev client's
    own blank state. `blank` conflates "empty" with "unowned".

6.4 The ordering defect the guard hides: in an enabling commit the plane update
    runs first, so a client frame can already be staged/flipped before
    `pipe_enable()` sets `scanout_offset = 0` (:3695) and reprograms the mode
    with offset 0. The frame is then either clobbered by the paint (when the
    card is painted) or dropped behind a scanout that was reset to the front
    buffer. Any fix that only removes the `fresh` flag without addressing
    :3695 re-exposes that: the enabling commit's frame would be uploaded to the
    back buffer, and the paint would still overwrite the front buffer that
    `program_mode` just selected. That interaction is asserted but not proven
    by this failure (the readback cannot distinguish "dropped by the guard" from
    "uploaded then clobbered"), so it is filed as a second defect rather than as
    the cause of this one.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. RECOMMENDED FIX
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Recommended single mechanism: (R1) - the driver cancels the test card and
refuses to paint it again whenever a CRC source is armed. A CRC consumer is by
definition a client that wants a stable frame; the driver must not write,
rotate or re-arm a card into a window it is feeding to that consumer. This is
the only option that fixes real clients, not just this harness (section 8), and
it needs no change to any script or test.

R1, exact call sites, all in src/sm750_drm.c:

  R1.1 `sm750_crc_enable()` :3251-3278 - between `sdev->crc_enabled = true;`
       (:3270) and `ret = sm750_crc_arm(sdev);` (:3272), call
       `sm750_test_pattern_cancel(sdev);`. Arming must also stop, not merely
       suppress, an already-running rotation, because
       `test_pattern_cancel()` is the only thing that performs
       `cancel_delayed_work_sync` (:3632). Keep the existing `crc_mutex`
       critical section; the cancel does not take `mode_lock`, so no new lock
       order is introduced.

  R1.2 `sm750_test_pattern_should_paint()` :3665-3671 - first statement:
       `if (READ_ONCE(sdev->crc_enabled)) return false;`. This covers both the
       `auto` and the `always` modes; the parameter is the module option and
       "always" must not override an armed CRC consumer. Document the reason in
       the function's leading comment: a CRC source disables the card for its
       lifetime.

  R1.3 `sm750_pipe_enable()` paint block :3781-3798 - with R1.2 in place the
       gate already returns false, so the block can stay as is; the belt-and-
       braces alternative is `if (sm750_test_pattern_should_paint(blank)
       && !READ_ONCE(sdev->crc_enabled))`. Do **not** add a second drm_info
       suppression path: silence is the correct behaviour here.

  R1.4 `sm750_test_pattern_work()` :3640-3655 - inside the existing
       `mode_lock` section, change the condition to
       `if (sdev->test_pattern_active && sdev->scanout_pitch &&
       !READ_ONCE(sdev->crc_enabled))`. This closes the window where
       `queue_delayed_work` had already fired and is waiting for `mode_lock`
       while `sm750_crc_enable()` cancels; without it one last paint can still
       land after the cancel returned (the `cancel_delayed_work_sync` only
       covers work that has not started running).

  R1.5 `sm750_crc_disable()` :3194-3199 - clear nothing about the card: the
       card stays off until the next `pipe_enable()`, which is the only place
       that knows the geometry and the blank state. Repainting on disable would
       contradict R1.2's "off for the consumer's lifetime" and could paint over
       a frame the consumer just left on screen. State this explicitly in the
       commit message so a later reader does not "fix" it.

  R1.6 `sm750_test_pattern_cancel()` :3627-3632 - no signature change needed;
       with R1.2/R1.4 it is now called from three places that matter:
       `pipe_disable()` :3925, `pci_remove()` :4669 and
       `sm750_crc_enable()` :3271.

  R1.7 `sm750_crc_suspend()` :3209 - it keeps `crc_enabled` (on purpose, the
       stream must survive a modeset), so R1.2 keeps the card suppressed across
       a modeset that happens while a consumer is armed. Verify with a test:
       arm the source, force a modeset, assert no paint line appears.

R1 verification that must be added (these are checkable without hardware for
the code path and with hardware for the effect):

  * tests/check-test-pattern.sh gains a planted negative control: remove R1.1
    and R1.2 and assert the CRC-instability failure comes back. The suite
    already has nine controls in that style
    (docs/evidence/runtime/23-test-card-fallback.txt section 9).
  * tests/check-scanout-crc.sh needs no change; it must pass unchanged, and the
    dump must read `nonzero 0` / `crc32 0xe20eea22` for the black phase on a
    load that painted a card before the client attached. That assertion already
    exists at :219-228 - it is the assertion that caught this defect, and it must
    not be weakened.

Optional companion fix, same defect class, recommended but separable:

  R2  Remove `test_pattern_fresh` entirely (:408-409 field, :3792 set,
      :3873-3876 guard, :3630 clear) and replace the paint trigger with
      "no userspace master": compute
      `bool unowned = !READ_ONCE(sdev->drm.master);` at :3767 and pass it to
      `should_paint()` alongside the blank state (e.g. `blank && unowned` for
      `auto`). With R1 in place this is not needed to make CRC stable, but
      without it the driver still drops a real client's first all-black frame
      and paints a card over a legitimately black desktop (6.3).

  R3  Separate defect, file it rather than fix it here: `pipe_enable()` resets
      `scanout_offset = 0` (:3695) after the enabling commit's plane update has
      already run (F15), so an enabling commit's frame can be clobbered or
      dropped. Needs its own evidence (a client whose first frame is non-blank
      arriving in the same commit as an enable) before a fix is chosen.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. ALTERNATIVES, RANKED, WITH TRADE-OFFS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. R1 (driver cancels and suppresses the card while a CRC source is armed).
   Chosen.
   + Fixes every CRC consumer, not just this harness: an IGT `kms_crc` style
     test on this driver would hit the same defect - it arms `scanout`, then
     presents a frame, the driver paints a card over it, the rotation rewrites
     the window, and the test fails with exactly this message. It also fixes the
     narrower real bug that a card survives a client's first commit (6.2).
   + Keeps T37's purpose intact: the card still paints when no CRC consumer
     exists, i.e. exactly the "console is blank, nobody paints" case it was
     written for, which is the normal case on this host (F22).
   + Small, local, no new locks, no script or test change, ASCII only.
   - It couples the card to the CRC feature: enabling a debugfs/IGT source
     blanks the panel's card. That is intended (a consumer owns the frame), but
     it must be documented or an operator will read it as a regression.
   - It does not by itself fix 6.3/R2 - a black *client* frame still gets
     painted over when no CRC source is armed. Hence R2 as a companion.

2. R2 alone (paint only when no userspace master exists, drop the fresh flag).
   Not sufficient, would be a good companion.
   + Removes the dropped-frame mechanism and the false "blank means nobody".
   - A CRC consumer arms the source while still being a userspace master that
     submits no new frames (exactly the check's `sleep`), so the card would
     still be repainted every 5 s into the sampled window. Does not fix this
     failure on its own.

3. (iv) Move the paint out of `pipe_enable()` into a work item that cannot run
   while a CRC source is armed.
   Rejected as the fix; orthogonal.
   + Would make the enable path cheaper and could serialise paint against
     uploads.
   - "Cannot run while a CRC source is armed" *is* R1.1/R1.2/R1.4; moving the
     paint adds a delayed step whose timing is harder to reason about, does not
     fix the dropped first frame, and does not fix the rotation (F7) which is
     already a work item.

4. (ii) `insmod src/sm750hdmidrm.ko test_pattern=off` in
   /usr/local/sbin/sm750-crc-cycle.
   Rejected.
   + One line, zero driver risk, restores the pre-T37 result immediately.
   - It only fixes the harness. The defect bites any client that arms CRC
     (IGT, `igt_kms` CRC checks, a future self-test), so the check would keep
     passing while the driver stays broken. It also silently disables a T37
     feature in the only automated run that exercises it, so the card would
     become untested in the very cycle that is supposed to cover it.
   - It cannot be used as the "before" leg of a regression test: the check would
     no longer exercise the interaction it just failed on.

5. (iii) Have `tests/check-scanout-crc.sh` flip `test_pattern` to `off` through
   sysfs before measuring and restore it afterwards.
   Rejected, and worse than (ii).
   + Leaves the cycle script untouched and keeps the card on screen before and
     after the measurement.
   - It encodes the driver's internal conflict into a test, so the test now
     asserts "the card is off" instead of asserting "the driver does not break
     CRC consumers". Any real client still fails.
   - The restore-on-exit path adds a new teardown ordering problem (the card
     would be re-enabled while the CRC source may still be armed, or while the
     check's client is exiting and triggering the F20 restore commit), i.e. it
     recreates the same race with an extra step.

6. Stop the rotation but keep the paint. Rejected as insufficient: it fixes the
   steady-state repaint (a) but not the paint performed by every later fbdev
   re-enable (section 5, F21), which is an independent repaint source and is
   exactly the one the dmesg pair at 22:13:33/22:13:39 shows on this host.

6'. Suppress CRC entries while a paint is running. Rejected: it hides the tear
   instead of preventing it, and it drops frames a consumer is entitled to
   (`drm_crtc_add_crc_entry` with a frame counter that must be contiguous).
   The consumer asked for the scanout's content; the correct answer is "the
   content is stable because nobody is painting it", not "the content is
   sometimes unavailable".

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. DOES THIS BITE A REAL CLIENT?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Yes, and the fix must therefore live in the driver.

An IGT `kms_crc` style test does exactly what tests/check-scanout-crc.sh does:
enable the CRC source on the CRTC, present or leave one frame, read
`crc/data` for N frames, assert a single distinct value, then compare against a
reference. On this driver such a client hits all of:
  * the card painted at enable time because the plane buffer it starts from is
    blank (F3, F4) - a client that arms CRC before its first present, which is
    the normal IGT order, gets the card, not its own frame;
  * the `test_pattern_fresh` guard dropping that client's first present (L3);
  * the rotation repainting the armed window every 5 s (F7);
  * the fbdev client's own re-commits repainting it again seconds apart (F21,
    section 5) - with no userspace action at all.

Only a driver-side rule "an armed CRC source stops the card" covers these. A
script flag or a sysfs flip in one test file changes nothing for any other
client, and the next person to add a CRC test would rediscover this failure.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. RESIDUAL UNCERTAINTY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

OPEN-1 Which fbdev-client path issued the 22:13:39 commit (hotplug re-probe or
       a restore after a probe retry). Does not change the mechanism or the fix;
       the command that closes it is in section 5.

OPEN-2 Whether the enabling commit's frame is *dropped* by the guard or
       *uploaded and then clobbered* by :3695. Both are covered by R1 (no paint)
       and R2 (no guard); the distinguishing experiment is a client whose first
       frame is non-blank presented in the same commit as an enable, with the
       card off. Filed as R3, needs its own evidence.

NOT CLAIMED: that the torn read is the *only* way to get three values. A
staged flip during the sample (b) would also give three; it did not happen in
this run, but R1 prevents it as well because the card - and therefore the
unwanted in-place repaint - is no longer present. The protection for (b)
properly belongs to the CRC consumer's own "no pending commit" contract, which
is out of scope here.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11. WHAT WOULD HAVE CAUGHT THIS EARLIER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

T37's evidence already declared the interaction unverified
(docs/evidence/runtime/23-test-card-fallback.txt sections 8-9: "a client commit
repaints over the card without a visible torn frame"). The gap was not the code
path but the missing pairing: `tests/check-test-pattern.sh` exercises the card
alone and `tests/check-scanout-crc.sh` exercises CRC alone, and no test ran the
two together on a load that painted a card. The cheapest permanent guard is the
planted negative control named in section 7 plus one combined check: arm the
source, assert the card is gone (no paint line since the arm, dump nonzero 0),
then disable the source and assert the card returns on the next enable.
